1

What they are

An LLM, a VLM and an SLM are the same kind of model. Each one predicts the next token.

An LLM reads text, a VLM also reads pages and images, and an SLM is a smaller model prepared for one job

2

How they differ

They differ in size, in what they can read and in what they cost to run.

About 300B, 30B and 7B parameters; a 7B model is 10–30x cheaper to serve than a 70–175B model

3

What each is suited to

Large models suit reasoning and drafting, vision models suit documents, small models suit narrow work at volume.

A research note from filings; a KYC pack; email triage

4

OpenAI kept one design and made it about 1,500 times larger, and one model began to handle many tasks

GPT-1

2018 · 117 million parameters

Trained again for each task

It first learned to predict the next token from books. Each task then needed a second round of training on labelled examples.

GPT-2

2019 · 1.5 billion parameters

Tested on tasks it was never trained for

Reading comprehension, translation and summarising, with no task training. The paper was titled "Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners".

GPT-3

2020 · 175 billion parameters

Given the task in the prompt

A few examples in the prompt were enough for translation, question answering and three-digit arithmetic. The model itself was not changed.

The training goal was the same in all three: predict the next token. What changed was the size, and the task moved out of the training data and into the prompt.

Circle area in proportion to parameter count. Sources: Radford et al. (OpenAI, 2018 and 2019); Brown et al. (OpenAI, 2020)

Emergent abilities: GPT-3 scored 0% on three-digit addition at 3 billion parameters and 76% at full size

Three-digit additions answered exactly right

"An ability is emergent if it is not present in smaller models but is present in larger models."

Wei et al., Google, 2022

Addition was not a training goal

Every size was trained only to predict the next token.

The size at which it appears differs

Google's models scored 0% at 27 billion parameters and 52% at 128 billion.

The loss curve gave no sign of it

Loss falls smoothly with size while single tasks move in steps.

Data: BIG-bench results, three-digit addition, zero-shot, exact match; largest GPT-3 plotted at 175B. Definition: Wei et al. (2022)

Emergence is a limit on prediction, so each new model version needs a test on your own work

The jump depends on how you score

Stanford researchers scored each digit separately in 2023 and found a smooth rise. The step appears when all four digits of the answer must be right together.

A business scores all-or-nothing

A NAV or a PAN with one wrong digit is a wrong NAV or PAN. The strict measure is the one that matters in operations.

"A language model that can almost perform a task is not much more useful than one that can't perform it at all."

A test confirms an ability; it cannot rule one out

Asking a model to "think step by step" raised its maths scores with no retraining. Abilities can surface after release.

For the AMC: treat a vendor upgrade as a new model and rerun your own test set before it reaches a process.

"These developments are neither magical nor inexplicable: we just have limited ability to predict them."

Thomas Woodside, CSET, Georgetown University, April 2024

A small model is the same design with fewer parameters, prepared for one job

Distillation

A large model answers thousands of questions, and the small model is trained to give the same answers.

Fine-tuning

Your own solved cases teach it one task. One study of financial tables needed 2,152 examples.

Quantisation

14 GB

3.5 GB

Each parameter is stored in 4 bits instead of 16, so a 7B model shrinks to a quarter of the memory.

What you give up is breadth. Outside its own task a small model gets worse quickly, so each one needs its own test set and its own owner.

Memory is for the weights alone: 7 billion parameters at 2 bytes and at half a byte each. Fine-tuning example: arXiv 2508.05669

An agent is a model working in a loop with tools, and the three models take different roles

Plan

decide the next step

Act

call a tool or system

Read

take in what came back

Check

done, retry, or ask a person

Large model: the planner

Breaks the goal into steps, chooses what to do next, and deals with the case nobody expected.

Small models: the workers

Do the narrow steps that repeat thousands of times: pick the tool, fill in its fields, classify the reply.

Vision model: the eyes

Reads whatever comes back as a page, a scan or a screen, and turns it into fields the others can use.

Most steps in an agent are narrow and repeated. NVIDIA's researchers argue these belong on small models, at 10–30x lower serving cost.

Source: NVIDIA, Small Language Models are the Future of Agentic AI (2025)
